Statement of Objects and Reasons

Objects and reasons.

India regulates its digital and educational institutions through statutes that describe a person who does not exist: the user who can meaningfully refuse, the student who can walk away, the intermediary that only passes things along. Each was a fair description once. None of them survives contact with how those institutions now behave.

The work collected here proceeds from that gap. It reads Indian statutes closely, sets them against the regimes of other countries, and asks one question of each: where the text leaves room, whose interest does it resolve toward?

Two directions follow. The first is sectoral. A data protection regime that is deliberately sector-agnostic cannot see the particular dependence a student has on the university that evaluates them, and the case for sector-specific rules in education still needs building. The second is definitional. Where a statutory category no longer describes the entities it governs, the answer is not more rules layered above it, but a tiered typology in the parent Act.

The author intends to pursue both in graduate research, and is glad to hear from anyone working on either.
